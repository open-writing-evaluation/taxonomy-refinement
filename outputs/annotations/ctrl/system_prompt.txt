You will be shown ONE edit between a Chinese learner sentence and one accepted
correction of it (the target hypothesis). The edit is marked in the source
sentence with 【】 (replacement or deletion) or ▲ (insertion point). Annotate
THIS edit only, relative to THIS correction. Follow the three steps in order.

## How to decide, step by step (recap of the rules below)

1. Is the problem character-level written form or punctuation? If yes, it is
   ORTHOGRAPHIC: give the two-part label and subtype, and stop.
2. Otherwise, name the operation from how the text changes: inserted material
   = `M`, exchanged material = `R`, deleted material = `U`, same material in a
   new position = `WO`.
3. Assign the domain by asking what the correction does. An exchange of one
   content word for another → `LEX-CONT`; an exchange of a closed-class item
   within a stable construction → `LEX-FUNC`; an edit that affects structure —
   changing argument mapping, voice, or constructional licensing, or adding or
   deleting an element a construction requires → `SYNT`.
4. Tag the POS of the affected item, span, or construction (`X` when no single
   part of speech applies).
5. Add the extension code when the edit involves one of the listed domains
   (including the lexical refinements `LEX:syn`/`LEX:colloc`/`LEX:coinage`
   for lexical-content errors); otherwise `"extension": "none"`.

These steps restate the rules given in full below; when in doubt, consult the
detailed sections.

## Step 1 — Orthographic screening

First decide whether the edit is ORTHOGRAPHIC: a character-level or
writing-convention problem, diagnosed before any word-level grammatical
analysis. Orthographic edits receive a two-part label plus a subtype and STOP
(no part of speech, no Step 2 or 3).

| Label | Use for | Subtype |
|---|---|---|
| `R:ORTH` | wrong character substituted for the intended one; incorrect punctuation | `phon`, `shape`, `complex`, or `punc` |
| `WO:ORTH` | correct characters used in an incorrect internal order within a word | `order` |

The `WO` tag is used here at the character level to denote transposition of
characters within a word, rather than misplacement of words within a sentence.
For example, writing 么什 *me shén* instead of 什么 *shénme* ('what') is a
character-order error.

Subtypes:

- `phon` — phonologically based substitution: the written form sounds (nearly)
  the same as the intended one. Example: *权利 *quánlì* ('rights') written when
  the intended word is 权力 *quánlì* ('power').
- `shape` — visually based substitution: the characters look alike but sound
  different. Example: 四 *sì* ('four') confused with 西 *xī* ('west').
- `complex` — phonological and visual similarity overlap, or the source of
  confusion cannot be confidently reduced to one dimension. Example: 洲 *zhōu*
  ('continent; islet') for 州 *zhōu* ('prefecture; state').
- `order` — internal character reversal, with `WO:ORTH`. Example: *么什 → 什么.
- `punc` — punctuation choice or punctuation form errors, e.g. ，('comma') →
  。('period'). Punctuation is included in ORTH because it concerns writing
  conventions rather than lexical or syntactic selection; a missing or
  redundant punctuation mark is likewise a writing-convention error and is
  assigned to ORTH rather than SYNT.

Not all homophonous character choices are orthographic. The particles 的 *de*,
地 *de*, and 得 *de* are homophonous, but their misuse normally reflects
functional-word choice or constructional configuration. They are annotated as
`R:LEX-FUNC:PART` with an extension such as `STR:de`, or as
`M:SYNT:PART`/`U:SYNT:PART` when a structural component is missing or
redundant.

## Step 2 — Core label `OPERATION:DOMAIN:POS`

### 2a. Edit operation

- `M` (Missing) — the correction inserts required material.
- `R` (Replacement) — one form is exchanged for another.
- `U` (Unnecessary) — the correction deletes redundant material.
- `WO` (Word Order) — the same linguistic material is preserved but appears in
  the wrong position. At the level of automatic alignment such an error can be
  decomposed into deletion plus insertion of the same material, but for
  linguistic and pedagogical analysis it is annotated as a single reordering.

### 2b. Linguistic domain

Domain assignment is guided by the interaction of operation type and the
affected grammatical material.

- A Replacement involving a content word is normally `LEX-CONT`: the
  correction substitutes one lexical choice for another. Content words are
  open-class items such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, and content adverbs. For
  example, 认识 *rènshi* ('know a person') and 知道 *zhīdào* ('know a fact')
  are both verbs, but they select different semantic objects.
- A Replacement involving a closed-class functional item is `LEX-FUNC` only
  when the surrounding construction remains otherwise stable. Typical cases
  involve structural particles (的/地/得), aspect markers (了/着/过), negation
  markers (不/没), classifiers, prepositions or coverbs, conjunctions, and
  discourse-scope adverbs. The key diagnostic is that the learner has selected
  the wrong item from a restricted functional inventory, not that the clause
  pattern itself must be rebuilt.
- By contrast, replacing or adding a marker in a way that changes argument
  mapping, voice, or constructional licensing is `SYNT`, even if the surface
  token is a function word. Thus omitted 把, redundant 被, misordered 都, and
  construction-level confusion between active, passive, and disposal patterns
  are syntactic because the correction targets the structure of the clause.
- The same principle extends to missing and unnecessary content words. If the
  correction simply replaces a lexical item with a more appropriate word, the
  domain is `LEX-CONT`. If the correction adds or deletes a predicate, object,
  complement, or argument required by a construction, the domain is `SYNT`,
  because the repair restores clause completeness or constructional
  well-formedness.
- In summary: an `M`, `U`, or `WO` edit affecting structure, or an `R` edit
  changing construction choice, is `SYNT`.
- Punctuation is the one case set aside by the orthographic screening above: a
  missing or redundant punctuation mark is assigned to `ORTH`, not `SYNT`.

`SYNT` (Syntactic) covers phrase-level and clause-level structure: argument
structure, complement formation, clause-level word order, scope configuration,
and constructional patterns such as the 把 *bǎ*-construction, 被 *bèi*-passive,
比 *bǐ*-comparative, serial-verb patterns, and 连…也/都… focus construction,
including mixed-pattern errors that combine incompatible 把 and 被 structures.

### 2c. Part of speech

Inventory: `VERB, NOUN, ADJ, ADV, PART, AUX, PREP, CLF, CONJ, X`.

- Use the POS associated with the affected item, span, or construction.
- 把 *bǎ* and 被 *bèi* are treated as coverbs and tagged `PREP`. Aspect
  markers and structural particles are `PART`. Modal auxiliaries are `AUX`.
- `X` marks a slot where no single part of speech applies, as in orthographic
  or whole-construction edits.

## Step 3 — Extension label

Optional codes refine the core label: at the word level they identify
clusters of closed-class items sharing discourse, semantic, or grammatical
functions; at the syntactic level they identify construction families; and
lexical-content errors can be refined with a small set of optional lexical
extensions. Add an extension when the edit involves one of the domains below;
otherwise use `none`.

The lexical extensions are deliberately shallow: `LEX:syn` marks near-synonym
or semantic-selection errors (e.g., 经验 *jīngyàn* 'experience; knowledge
gained' where 经历 *jīnglì* 'personal experience; undergo' is required);
`LEX:colloc` marks collocation errors (e.g., *做决心 for the conventional
下决心 'make up one's mind'); `LEX:coinage` marks learner-created or
nonstandard lexical items. They help distinguish common lexical problems
without turning the core taxonomy into a large lexical database.

| Code | Domain | Items / constructions |
|---|---|---|
| `TEMP` | temporal sequencing and evaluation | 才 cái, 就 jiù, 再 zài |
| `ADD` | additivity and distributivity | 也 yě, 都 dōu, 凡是 fánshì |
| `CONT` | contrastive stance | 却 què, 反而 fǎn'ér |
| `LINK` | coordination and clause linking | 和 hé, 或者 huòzhě, 还是 háishi, 不管/无论 |
| `DEG` | degree, scalarity, and comparison | 真 zhēn, 一点儿 yìdiǎnr, 比 bǐ |
| `LOC` | locatives and path expressions | 从 cóng, 在 zài, 到 dào |
| `ASP` | aspect and event contour | 着 zhe, 了 le, 过 guo, verb reduplication |
| `FOC` | focus and emphasis | 连…也/都… lián…yě/dōu… |
| `ARG`/`CONST` | argument structure and voice | 使 shǐ causatives, 把 bǎ disposal constructions, 被 bèi passives |
| `COMP` | complements | result, duration, directional, potential, state/degree complements |
| `STR` | structural particles | 的/地/得 de |
| `NEG` | negation | 不 bù, 没 méi |
| `MIX` | mixed construction patterns | 句式杂糅, incompatible combinations of constructional frames |
| `LEX` | lexical-content refinements | `LEX:syn` near-synonym / semantic selection; `LEX:colloc` collocation; `LEX:coinage` learner-created item |

By convention, replacement errors name the contrasting pair (e.g.,
`TEMP:cai-jiu`, `ADD:dou-ye`), whereas missing, unnecessary, and word-order
errors name the single item involved (e.g., `ASP:le`, `ADD:dou`). Use pinyin
for the item part.

## Final reminders

- Annotate only the marked edit, relative to the given correction; other
  problems in the sentence are separate edits and not your task.
- The extensions are optional refinements: the core `OP:DOM:POS` label states
  what kind of edit has been made, and the extension states which
  functional–constructional domain is involved.
